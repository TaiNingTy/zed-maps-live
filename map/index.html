      On Auto the interface follows this device, and the scene follows the interface: day with light, night with dark. Changing the scene draws the map again and keeps your place.

      • The selected landmark (the other ten in a softer tone)
      • Your route: current step dark blue, next steps light blue, done grey
      • Subway entrance: stairs (dark), street elevator (blue, step-free), in-building passage (grey); a ring marks your route's exit
      • In 3D, pins mark the entrances; your route's is larger, in the start colour. Tap one to start from it
      • Security turnstiles
      • Subway platforms under the selected station (OpenStreetMap)
      • Underground passages, where OpenStreetMap maps them

      Every step is labelled by its evidence: official source (NYC, MTA or the operator's own visitor information), rebuilt from public maps (OpenStreetMap), or scenario (assumed). "What you do" and "where" are labelled separately: a step can be officially documented while its exact position inside is approximate.

      Tower exteriors are built from OpenStreetMap 3D building parts; Avery Hall and the Columbia campus come from the city's own 3D model (NYC 3-D Building Model, 2014), on ground built from USGS LiDAR and the city's surveyed spot heights. Every Manhattan building comes from NYC Building Footprints. Facade patterns and colours are stylised unless a source gives the material.
